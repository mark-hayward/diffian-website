The problem

Generic speech engines fail in Welsh.

Mixed-language sentences

Real service content switches between Welsh and English constantly. Single-language voices read one of them wrong every time.

Fi: the language is detected word by word, so each word is read in its own language by one natural voice.

Place names

Llanelli, Pen-y-lan, Cwmbrân. The Welsh ll (ɬ) is a consonant English simply doesn’t contain, and Welsh speakers notice instantly.

Fi: a national lexicon of Welsh place names, with the genuine Welsh sounds.

Mutations

Welsh words change their first letter with grammar: Caerdydd becomes Gaerdydd or Nghaerdydd. Dictionary-form engines get ordinary sentences wrong.

Fi: pronunciation entries cover the mutated forms explicitly.

Use our voice, or own your own. Start with Carys, ready today. Or we train a bespoke voice for your organisation from recordings of a speaker you choose: one voice, your brand, speaking English and Welsh, with your place and service names said properly in both.

Where Fi fits

Health boards

Appointment reminders, outpatient queue calling, spoken patient information, Welsh-first telephone services. Patient-derived text never has to leave your network: the service can run entirely on your own infrastructure, which materially simplifies the DPIA.

Councils & public bodies

Public transport
