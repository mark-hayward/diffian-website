The problem

Generic speech engines fail in Welsh.

Welsh isn’t English with different words. Real service content mixes two languages in a single sentence, and the big cloud voices weren’t built for it. When we last checked, none of the major vendors would even train a custom voice that speaks Welsh.

Mixed-language sentences

Announcements, IVR menus and service messages switch between Welsh and English constantly. Single-language voices read one of them wrong every time.

Diffian: the language is detected word by word, so each word is read in its own language by one natural voice.

Place names

Llanelli, Pen-y-lan, Aberystwyth, Cwmbrân. English voices mangle Welsh place names, and Welsh speakers notice instantly.

Diffian: a curated lexicon of thousands of Welsh place names, built from national transport data.

Mutations

Welsh words change their first letters with grammar. Caerdydd becomes Gaerdydd or Nghaerdydd depending on context, and engines that only know dictionary forms get real sentences wrong.

Diffian: pronunciation entries cover mutated forms, with per-word overrides where rules fall short.

What we built

Our own voice. Our own engine. Built for Welsh from the ground up.

Fi is the engine and Carys is its voice. When we found that no cloud vendor could give us a custom voice that speaks Welsh, we trained our own. Carys is one neural voice that speaks both languages, so her English says Welsh place names properly and her Welsh flows like a first language. And because the voice belongs to us, it can belong to your service: no per-character licensing and no vendor lock-in.

Carys, a voice you can own

A custom-trained neural voice producing 24 kHz audio, efficient enough to run on ordinary CPU servers. Self-host it on your own infrastructure or consume it as a managed API. Your content never has to leave your estate.

Bilingual by design

Per-word language detection handles code-switching automatically, with explicit control when you need it. One request, one voice, both languages, correctly.

Fine control

An SSML subset covers pauses, prosody, substitutions and exact phoneme overrides, plus Welsh and English number expansion. Your Welsh language officer’s corrections can be applied precisely and regenerated in seconds.

Delivery that fits your hardware

A simple HTTP API for live generation, or pre-generated offline audio bundles for annunciators, queue systems and kiosks. GPS-triggered on-vehicle playback needs no connectivity at all.

Contact centres & IVR

Few organisations can staff every phone line and automated touchpoint with Welsh speakers. Give your IVR menus, queue messages and automated lines a fluent Welsh-first voice, without recruiting for it.

Health boards

Appointment reminders and automated calls, outpatient queue calling, spoken patient information and Welsh-first telephone services. Patient-derived text never has to leave your network: CPU-only self-hosting materially simplifies the DPIA.

Public transport

The Public Service Vehicles (Accessible Information) Regulations 2023 require audible next-stop announcements on local bus services, with deadlines from October 2026. Diffian generates complete bilingual announcement sets packaged for standard on-vehicle annunciator hardware.

Councils & public bodies

Councils, national parks and museums: tannoy announcements, wayfinding, queue systems and digital services, generated in both languages from a single source, with every place name in your area said properly.

Digital services

Read-aloud on public-facing web content and apps, so a Welsh speaker gets Welsh audio rather than an English voice attempting it.
