The problem

Generic speech engines fail in Welsh.

Welsh isn’t English with different words. Real service content mixes two languages in a single sentence, and the big cloud voices weren’t built for it. When we last checked, none of the major vendors would even train a custom voice that speaks Welsh.

Mixed-language sentences

Announcements, IVR menus and service messages switch between Welsh and English constantly. Single-language voices read one of them wrong every time.

Diffian: Welsh place names inside English sentences are read the Welsh way, by one natural voice.

Place names

Llanelli, Pen-y-lan, Aberystwyth, Cwmbrân. English voices mangle Welsh place names, and Welsh speakers notice instantly.

Diffian: a curated lexicon of Welsh place names, built from national transport data.

Mutations

Welsh words change their first letters with grammar. Caerdydd becomes Gaerdydd or Nghaerdydd depending on context, and engines that only know dictionary forms get real sentences wrong.

Diffian: pronunciation entries cover mutated forms, with per-word overrides where rules fall short.
